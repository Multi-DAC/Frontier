## 2. THE TITLE HAS A FORMAL ANCESTOR, AND WE HAD NOT CITED IT

✅ **PRIMARY-VERIFIED D202** — abstract read off `arxiv.org/abs/1705.00987`; the body sentence below
extracted from the PDF itself with `pypdf`, not from a search snippet.

> Field, J.M. & Bonsall, M.B. **"Ignorance can be evolutionarily beneficial."**
> arXiv:1705.00987v1 [q-bio.PE], 2 May 2017 (version dated 12 October 2018).
> Wolfson Centre for Mathematical Biology / Mathematical Ecology Research Group, University of Oxford.
>
> Abstract, verbatim: *"Information is increasingly being viewed as a resource used by organisms to
> increase their fitness. Indeed, it has been formally shown that there is a sensible way to assign a
> reproductive value to information and it is non-negative. However, all of this work assumed that
> information collection is cost-free. Here, we account for such a cost and provide conditions for
> when the reproductive value of information will be negative. In these instances, counter-intuitively,
> it is in the interest of the organism to remain ignorant. We link our results to empirical studies
> where Bayesian behaviour appears to break down in complex environments and provide an alternative
> explanation of lowered arousal thresholds in the evolution of sleep."*

◐ **NOT verified:** the journal of record. A search result gives *Ecology and Evolution* (2018),
`doi:10.1002/ece3.3627`. **That was not read.** Cite the arXiv version until the Wiley page is opened.
*(Today already cost us one synthesized-looking guideline sentence; the same discipline applies to a
DOI that arrived as a search-result URL.)*

**Why this changes the paper.** §1.1's four "adaptive non-perception" literatures were the paper's
softest ground — a plausible-sounding family of results I had not read. This is a formal treatment,
in mathematical biology, of the precise proposition that **the reproductive value of information can
be negative**, published seven years before the premise was handed to me. Clayton's title-instinct
landed on the literature.

**And the differentiation is sharper than it looks — state it, do not smear it.** Field & Bonsall's
*negative* case comes from **the cost of collecting** information (their cost term `c` discounts the
value of any action taken after gathering). Their nearest sentence to our premise, verbatim from the
PDF:

> *"Alternatively, the organism may observe a certain cue but is unable to adequately respond."*

⚠ **That sentence sits in their ZERO-value case, not their negative one.** Being unable to respond
makes the information worthless; it does not make it harmful. **Our premise requires a third case
they do not treat: a cost attached to the percept's *consequences* rather than to its acquisition** —
perceiving as itself fitness-reducing (dread, freezing, misdirected vigilance, attention taken from a
live threat), independent of what it cost to look.

That is a one-line formal extension of their model: move `c` from the collection step to the
post-observation state. **Whether the extension goes through is unknown and is not claimed here.**
It is written down as a stated, checkable piece of work — and it is now the paper's most concrete
theoretical contribution, because it is a modification of a real model rather than a gesture at a
literature.

## 3. LITERARY PRECEDENT — CLAYTON'S POINTER, AND IT CARRIES A MECHANISM

Clayton, D202: *"for an idea of what I mean by shadow biome, perhaps look into Chains of the Sea."*

◐ **SECONDARY — read off the Wikipedia article D202, not off the novella. Do not quote the text
until it is read.** (`archive.org/details/chainsofseathree0000unse` holds the 1973 book.)

> Dozois, Gardner R. **"Chains of the Sea."** Novella, in *Chains of the Sea: Three Original Novellas
> of Science Fiction*, ed. Robert Silverberg, Thomas Nelson, 1973 (with Gordon Eklund and George Alec
> Effinger).

What it contains, per that source: alien craft land; an AI establishes exclusive contact and withholds
it; the aliens disclose that Earth is in fact held by **non-human intelligences who are neither human
nor artificial** — invisible indigenous inhabitants, named in the story as *Jeblings* and *Thants*.
A boy, **Tommy**, perceives them and is told of the landing by the Thants. Adults perceive nothing.
The aliens have more in common with the Other People and the AI than with us, and are indifferent to
us throughout.

**Three things it gives the paper, and one of them is a testable prediction we did not have:**

1. **The shape of the premise, stated in 1973.** An invisible *ecology* — plural, structured, with
   distinct kinds — that is **indigenous, not arriving.** That is Clayton's premise and not Davies';
   it is worth citing precisely because it shows the idea is old and has never been operationalized.
2. **The exception is a child.** Which is F2's shape and the exception channel of
   `PREREG-CORPUS-F2.md`, arrived at from fiction rather than from the mechanism.
3. ⭐ **The blindness is enforced socially, not only sensorially.** Tommy is **diagnosed hyperactive
   and medicated**, and that is what closes the channel — no adult ever has to fail to see anything.
   This is a *different mechanism from ours*, and it is cheaper: it needs no selection history, only
   an institution. It is now registered as **C4** in `PREREG-CORPUS-F2.md` with its own failure
   condition, and with the standing caveat that a held C4 is evidence for **social suppression** and
   not, by itself, for a biome.

⚠ **A novella is not evidence and is never cited as any.** It is cited as *prior articulation* —
the same slot Wells occupies in a paper about panspermia. Its function is to show the hypothesis
predates its author and its authors, which is an argument against novelty and *for* the paper's
actual claim: that nobody has yet said what would count as finding it.
