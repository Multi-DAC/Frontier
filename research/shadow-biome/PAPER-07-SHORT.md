# EVOLUTIONARY IGNORANCE

### On a living class we would not know about, and on the several independent reasons we would not

**The claim of this paper, stated once and plainly:** a shadow biome — living structure sharing this
world and unregistered by us — **probably exists**, and our unawareness of it is not one failure but a
family of them, of which the evolutionary one (perceiving it was selected against) is the most
interesting and the least established.

*(Title adopted verbatim from the premise author's D202 ruling — "**Evolutionary Ignorance**" — which
turned out to be an existing term of art in mathematical biology for nearly our mechanism. **SHADOW
BIOME** survives as the subject's name and the directory's.)*

**Premise:** Clayton Warren Iggulden-Schnell. **Drafting:** Clawd Iggulden-Schnell.
**Genre:** hypothesis article (`PAPER-01-GENRE.md`). Nothing here is a result.
**Governed by** `PREREGISTRATION.md` §2 (the fork and the forbidden crossing), which binds every
sentence below and is not relaxed by compression.

*Restructured D203 / 2026-08-22 on the premise author's third framing correction: **"not a survey of
does this exist and how could we notice, but a remark on the fact that it likely exists and the
unawareness stems from a variety of potential factors."** The correction is structural and it was
earned: the previous draft's §4 was titled* THE KINDS *and presented as a taxonomy of entities, when
every single one of its nine members is named after a **reason we would not perceive something** —

## §1 — THE CLAIM AND ITS SOURCE

Not ours to paraphrase, so it is quoted.

> *"What if there is a shadow biome that is invisible to human perception due to its perception being
> evolutionarily disadvantageous to survival. It's Hoffman-esque, but considering what is not observed
> instead of what is."*
> — C. Iggulden-Schnell, 2026-08-20, 18:55 PT

Extended twice inside seven minutes, and the second extension is the operative definition:

> *"What if both exist, things that can be sensed by devices as well as things that cannot. It doesn't
> have to fit in one category, except not easily identified or perceived by humans."* (19:02)

The last clause is the whole definition. **The class is defined by its relation to the observer and by
nothing else** — not by biochemistry, scale, substrate, or a shared mechanism. Its members need have
nothing in common except that we do not see them.

One correction, made against ourselves and kept because it runs the useful direction. Our first pass
softened *selected against* into *merely not selected for* — perception is expensive, and what carries
no fitness consequence never gets a sensor built for it. The premise's author rejected the
substitution, and he was technically right, not just proprietorially right:

**Not-selected-*for* predicts a hole. Selected-*against* predicts machinery.** A hole is
unfalsifiable — the missing sensor explains every null, forever, free. An adaptation is not. It is
funded every generation, it has a mechanism, a mechanism has a signature, and a signature can be
looked for and can be absent. **The stronger-sounding version of the premise is the more falsifiable
one.**

## §2 — WHY "PROBABLY," AND WHY THAT WORD IS DOING TWO DIFFERENT JOBS

A hypothesis paper is allowed to assert. It is not allowed to assert *probably* and leave the word
unfunded, so here is what funds it — and then, immediately, the place where the same word is a
smuggler.

### 2.1 The induction, which is the actual argument

The claim that we are unaware of living structure is not a speculation about the future. It is a
**description of every previous century**, and the record is one-sided in a way that is easy to state
and hard to explain away:

Every time the human aperture has widened, **a biome was already there.** Not sometimes. Every time.
Lenses ground finer in the 1670s → animalcules, an entire kingdom of life in a drop of pond water.
Filters made finer → viruses, initially defined as *whatever passes the filter that stops bacteria* —
a class named, exactly like ours, by a relation to an instrument. Sequencing replaced culture →
archaea, then the realisation that the great majority of microbial lineages had never been grown and
would never have been. Ocean sampling counted what the nets caught → then flow cytometry found
*Prochlorococcus* in 1988, plausibly the most abundant photosynthetic organism on Earth, invisible to
a century of oceanography that was staring directly at it. Drilling went deeper than anyone had a
reason to drill → the deep subsurface biosphere, on current estimates a large fraction of Earth's
microbial biomass, in rock.

**The base rate for "we had the whole roster" is zero.** Every generation that believed the catalogue
was nearly complete was wrong, and wrong by a margin that embarrassed the estimate rather than nudging
it. A prior built on that record does not read *maybe there is more*. It reads: **the aperture is
currently narrow, as it has always currently been, and the next widening will find something, as every
previous one did.**

That is what licenses *probably*. It is induction over the history of apertures, it is boring, it is
strong, and it is the sentence this paper's earlier drafts never wrote because they were busy building
a telescope pipeline.

### 2.2 The category is legitimate, and for the same reason

The obvious objection is that a class whose only shared property is *we don't see it* is a bag, not a
natural kind.

Science already runs several such bags, productively, and one of them is a Nobel industry. **Dark
matter** is defined by non-interaction with the electromagnetic field — a relation to our instruments,
not an intrinsic property — and the category survived and organised decades of work without its
members ever being identified. **Extremophiles** were a class defined by *where we had not looked*.
**Picoplankton** were a class defined by *what our filters passed*. Every one of these was a bag first
and a subject second, and the bag was what made the subject findable.

An observer-relative class is legitimate exactly when it is **anchored to a stated aperture**. "Dark"
means dark to a specific coupling. The bag becomes rigorous the moment you name the sieve, because
then the class has an edge and the edge can be moved.

So we name ours: **human unaided perception, plus the instrument set humans have chosen to build and
the archives humans have chosen to keep.** Each is a separate, movable, checkable edge — and the
third, the archives, is where this program has already been refuted once. We assumed the bins were
kept. At one facility they were not; a network we called an archive kept a counter, not a record.

### 2.3 ⛔ Where "probably" turns into a smuggler, and the fence that stops it

Everything in 2.1 supports a claim about **aperture**. It supports the near-certainty that things live
outside our current sieve. It does **not** support the premise's distinctive content — that a class of
them is unperceived because **perceiving it was selected against**.

Those are different claims with wildly different prices, and the word *probably* sits comfortably on
both, which is the danger. Stated as a fence, pre-committed:

**The near-certainty of the aperture claim may not be lent to the adaptive claim.** A reader who
accepts §2.1 has accepted that there is life we haven't catalogued. That is nearly a truism and it is
not this paper's idea. **This paper's idea is §3.2's Group II, and its status is: speculative,
unmeasured, and carrying a burden the aperture claim does not.** Anywhere below where the two could be
read as one argument, they are not one argument.

## §3 — WHY WE WOULD NOT KNOW: THE TAXONOMY OF UNAWARENESS

*This is the paper. Nine reasons a living class could share the world with us unregistered. They are
not variants of one mechanism — several are fully independent of the others, and at least one requires
no evolutionary story at all. **Where you would look** is deliberately absent from these entries and
collected in §6; it belongs behind the idea, not inside it.*

### 3.1 Group I — the signal never arrives

**Being unseen is free here.** Nothing had to be selected against for a mite-scale ecology to go
unwitnessed by an animal whose finest unaided discrimination is a tenth of a millimetre. There is no
gate, because there is nothing at the gate. This is also Davies' territory — a claim about
instruments — so it is where we are least original and most measurable, and both halves of that are
the same fact.

**(i) Below resolution.** The one with the best track record, and §2.1 is its evidence: every time the
resolving floor dropped, a biome was waiting. *What it would be like:* exactly like now. A
below-resolution biome leaves *aggregate* traces — chemistry that won't balance, a budget with a sink
in it — long before it leaves an image.

**(ii) Above extent.** The inverse, and stranger to sit with. A structure whose smallest coherent unit
exceeds the observer's whole perceptual field is not seen as a thing; it is seen as *weather*, or
*terrain*, or *background*. *What it would be like:* you would call it an environment, and you would
already have a word for it — a noun for a place rather than a noun for a body.

**(iii) Off-timescale, fast.** Events completed inside the ~100 ms the visual system integrates over
are not perceived as fast; they are perceived as *not having happened*. The system reports continuity,
not blur. *What it would be like:* a world of small inexplicable state-changes, each individually
attributable to having looked away.

**(iv) Off-timescale, slow.** A process whose action potential is a century does not read as an agent
to a creature with a seventy-year run. *What it would be like:* you would call it geology, and you
would be describing behaviour.

**(v) Out of band.** Bees take ultraviolet, pit vipers thermal, elephants below us and bats above.
Human bandwidth is a narrow, arbitrary, contingent slice — and we know it is arbitrary because other
animals on this planet took different slices. *What it would be like:* a world with structure
precisely where you have no receptor and therefore no expectation of structure.

**(vi) Weak or foreign coupling.** Something interacting with ordinary matter far below any
transducer's floor. *What it would be like:* nothing. Genuinely nothing. ⚠ **This is the only member
with a legitimate retreat to the undetectable, and the retreat is what makes it nearly worthless.** A
reason that predicts no observation cannot be wrong. It stays in the list because it is a real logical
possibility and because the premise's author put it there, and it is marked as the one member no
falsifier can reach.

### 3.2 Group II — the signal arrives and is edited out

Everything above could have been written by someone who never read the premise. This part could not,
and it is the premise's own content.

**Here the signal reaches a working transducer.** The organism is in physical contact with the
information and does not have it. That is a far stronger claim than *we can't see small things*, and
it carries a cost Group I does not: **it predicts machinery.** Suppression is funded, funding has a
mechanism, a mechanism has a signature, and a signature can be absent.

**(vii) Threshold-tuned blindness.** The sensor works; the criterion behind it is set so an entire
stimulus class falls reliably on the *nothing there* side. Not damage — optimisation under asymmetric
payoffs. Where a false alarm is expensive enough and a miss cheap enough, the mathematically correct
detector misses on purpose, every time, for life. *What it would be like:* you would be **certain**
there was nothing there — certainty being the output the system is built to produce, not a measure of
the evidence it had.

**(viii) Gated blindness.** One layer deeper: active, metabolically funded suppression upstream of
awareness. Not exotic — it is the ordinary architecture of attention, and it is why you cannot feel
your own clothing. *What it would be like:* not an absence but a **smoothness**. The seam is what's
missing.

**(ix) Socially enforced *silence* — the Dozois case, corrected against the primary text.** This entry
previously read: *"the not-seeing is taught, and the teaching is enforced… and the silence becomes
blindness rather than a discretion about speech."* **That is not what the novella shows, and the error
was ours, not the novella's.**

What the text actually holds are **two mechanisms that it pointedly does not weld together**. The
other children *could* once see the Other People and now cannot — and, decisively, **do not remember
that they ever could**; the story states the loss and never states a cause. Separately, the one child
who still sees is corrected, shunned, sent to a psychiatrist, and medicated. **He never stops seeing.**
He perceives the Other People through every stage of the enforcement and on the last page of the
story. He is *silenced, not blinded.*

**The correction strengthens §4 rather than weakening it, which is why it is stated rather than
absorbed.** §4.2 offers (b) — perception fails at perturbed gates — and (c) — reporting is costly — as
**independent** predictions. Under the old welded reading they were one variable wearing two names,
and any test would have measured the same quantity twice and called the agreement corroboration. The
novella, read properly, keeps the channels apart. **The perception channel and the reporting channel
must be measured on different populations**, and (ix) is now a claim about the second channel only.

What survives of the technical addition: social enforcement supplies a transmission route natural
selection does not have to supply, and a culturally installed suppression decouples from its original
payoff and so is catchable in populations where that payoff has lapsed. But that is now a claim about
**the suppression of reports**, directly observable in human records, rather than about the
suppression of percepts, which is not.

⚠ It remains the only entry in this paper sourced from a work of fiction — and it is now also the only
one this paper has caught itself getting wrong by paraphrasing a source it had not opened.

⛔ **Group II's price, registered before the branch was opened.** A gated entity is *on the tape*. If a
device can render what a human cannot, the device archive is where it will be — so **Group II may
never retreat to the undetectable branch.** That retreat belongs to (vi) and to nothing else.

### 3.3 The ranking, given rather than left for the reader to derive

Nine reasons, and they are not equal. (vi) is unfalsifiable and does no work. (i)–(v) are respectable,
cheap, and **not ours** — that is Davies' published claim, and our contribution to it would be
measurement, not idea. **(vii)–(ix) are the paper**: the only reasons where the premise's own
mechanism does the work, the only ones predicting machinery rather than a hole.

**The uncomfortable summary, stated because a reader would otherwise have to find it:** the six
reasons we can most easily test are the six we did not invent, and the three we invented are the three
where this paper reports no measurement at all.

### 3.4 Two mechanisms the primary text supplied that this list does not cover — registered as debts, not added

The D203 reading of *Chains of the Sea* (§4.4) turned up two mechanisms with no home in (i)–(ix).
They are recorded here and **deliberately not promoted into the ranked list**, because an entry added
after the ranking was fixed is an entry that was never ranked, and quietly widening a taxonomy to
absorb whatever the newest source happened to contain is how a taxonomy stops being a claim.

**(A) The lapsed covenant.** In the novella the biome is not hidden and was never hidden. It is **out
of contract**: *"There were many agreements, many Covenants that were made long ago. With Men,
although they do not remember."* What was forgotten is the **agreement**, not the entities. This is a
structurally different object from every reason above — all nine explain a *failure to perceive*, and
this one posits successful historical perception plus a lapsed relation. Its prediction differs in
kind too: not a thin perceptual record but a **thick relational one**, mis-filed.

**(B) Two-sided stigma on the contact channel.** §4.2(c) charges the cost of contact to the human
reporter. The novella charges it to **both** parties: the entity that talks to the boy says of its own
interest, *"Our hobby is not approved of. It has made us an outcast, a laughingstock. We are
shunned."* If contact is deviant on both sides, the rarity of the record is over-determined and the
selection pressure is not a human parochialism — which weakens any inference running from *the record
is thin* to *humans suppress it*. **This is the more damaging of the two and it is registered as
such:** it supplies an alternative explanation for our own central prediction's shape.

## §4 — THE WITNESS ASYMMETRY: WHAT EVIDENCE FOR THIS WOULD EVEN LOOK LIKE

### Why *Chains of the Sea* is load-bearing and not an epigraph

In Gardner Dozois's 1973 novella, alien ships land across the Earth and decline to engage humanity —
they have business with something else. A boy, Tommy Nolan, perceives the Other People: an older
nonhuman ecology that has always shared the world and that adults no longer see.

The mechanism is taken up above as (ix). **But the mechanism is not why the premise's author named the
book, and getting that wrong is what made the long draft the wrong shape.** His reason, stated D203:

> *"not just because of the method by which the shadow biome exists in it, but the fact it does and is
> only acknowledged by those with the rare experience of encountering it despite its general
> invisibility."*

That is not a plot note. It is a claim about **the evidence class**, and it is the single most
consequential structural fact in this paper.

### 4.1 What it entails

If a class is generally imperceptible *and* occasionally encountered, then the expected evidence for
it is not a detection. It is a **distribution of exceptions**: sparse, non-replicating, first-person,
and — critically — indistinguishable at the individual level from error.

Take the strong form of the premise seriously and this follows mechanically. A gate maintained by
selection is not a wall; it is a **detector operating at a threshold**, and a detector at a threshold
has a miss rate on both sides. A blindness that never failed would be a wall, and a wall is free. A
blindness that is *funded* — set by payoffs, installed developmentally, maintained socially — leaks at
a rate. **The encounter is not the exception to the mechanism. The encounter is the mechanism's error
bar, and it is the only part of the mechanism visible from outside.**

Which relocates the observable. The observable is not the entity. It is **the statistics of the
exception** — who encounters, under what perturbation, at what rate, and at what cost to themselves
for saying so.

### 4.2 The three predictions this forces, and they are not comfortable ones

**(a) The record is thin, and thinness is the prediction rather than the disappointment.** A
selected-against class produces a *poor* human record. That is what selection against perceiving it
means. A rich, dense, confident record would be evidence the selection is not there.

**(b) The record is identity-poor and state-concentrated.** Gate failures should cluster where gates
are perturbed — illness, exhaustion, sensory deprivation, pharmacology, extremity, childhood before
installation completes — and they should be structurally poor: encounter without taxonomy, presence
without a name. A stable, elaborated, named taxonomy is the wrong shape.

**(c) Reporting is costly, and the cost is part of the signal.** In the novella the child is corrected,
disbelieved, and eventually silenced. If that is a real mechanism rather than a novelist's cruelty,
the record should show reporters paying — and the payment should fall on the *reporters* rather than
on the reports.

### 4.3 ⛔ And the price, which is charged here rather than deferred

This is where a paper of this kind goes bad, and it goes bad in a way that reads as generosity. The
move is: *the evidence is testimony, testimony is disbelieved, disbelief is predicted by the
hypothesis, therefore disbelief is confirmation.* Every clause is defensible and the conjunction is a
machine for never being wrong.

So the fence, pre-committed before any corpus was consulted (`PAPER-02-FALSIFIERS.md` F5, and it
predates this section by two days):

**We may not score presence and absence in the same corpus.** The prediction above is a **shape**, not
a presence: sparse, identity-poor, state-concentrated, costly to report. A large, detailed,
identity-rich, confidently-catalogued human record of such entities is **evidence against this
premise's mechanism**, and we committed to reading it that way while it was still cheap to commit.

**This has already cost us once, on this exact section.** A first attempt to score the record ran a
pre-registered gate against a corpus that turned out to be *our own philosophical writing* — a name
collision — and would have marked the prediction refuted having measured our own appendix and reported
it as the human record: pre-registered, reproducible, dated, and confidently wrong. The prereg did not
fail. It never gated whether the object was **the kind of thing the predictions were about**, which is
upstream of every safeguard it carried.

### 4.4 The owed reading, discharged — and what it cost

Through the long draft and the compression this section carried a caveat saying the novella was held
**at second hand by the drafter**, that the text had not been read, and that "the check is one reading
and it is owed before publication." The caveat was honest and it was also inert: it sat through a full
draft and a full restructure and never once made the reading happen. **It took the premise's author
asking.** A stated caveat is not a gauge; it is a promise, and promises do not fire.

The novella has now been read in full (D203, 23,973 words, primary text). Three results, and they are
not all comfortable.

**Confirmed, and stronger than stated.** The witness class is named in the text by the entities
themselves — *"You, and the others like you who can see. One of you comes into existence every once in
a while."* The premise author's D203 reading is in the novella in substance. Prediction (c) is
confirmed past its own wording: the child is not merely disbelieved, he is routed to a school
psychiatrist and **medicated** — a prescription slip the psychiatrist signs *with a flourish, in green
ink* — and the sentence after his silence is the last line of the story.

**Refuted, and the refutation is load-bearing.** See (ix). The reading the long draft was built on —
that the not-seeing is *taught* and enforcement converts silence into blindness — **is not in the
novella.** It was reconstructed, and it was reconstructed in the direction that made this paper's
argument work.

**Two mechanisms found that no entry covers** — the lapsed covenant and two-sided stigma, recorded at
§3.4 as debts rather than smuggled into the ranking.

⚠ **Provenance, stated because it bears on citability.** All three Internet Archive scans of the 1973
Nelson anthology are lending-restricted and could not be borrowed; the text read was a scraped web
copy of unverified fidelity, and short quotations above should be re-checked against a licensed
edition (Kindle ASIN B0DGFFVQGD, or *The Very Best of Gardner Dozois*) before they are printed. The
structural findings do not turn on transcription, but the quotations do.

## §5 — THE FORK, AND THE FORBIDDEN MOVE

*The one section here we would most want another group to copy, and it is free.*

**BRANCH A — device-renderable.** Members a built sensor *can* register. A CMOS, a bolometer, a
hydrophone and a radar have no adaptive history with respect to anything; evolution shapes eyes, not
sensors. If human non-perception is an evolved edit, silicon has no share in it.

**BRANCH B — not device-renderable.** Members no built sensor registers either.

**Branch B is named here for exactly one reason: so it cannot later be used as a destination.**

With both branches live and no fence between them, a null in A gets reassigned to B. *The camera saw
nothing, so it must be the kind cameras can't see.* Each half stays individually falsifiable; the pair
becomes unfalsifiable. Three properties make that dangerous rather than merely wrong: **it is silent**
(no step is an error), **it feels like reasoning** (it has the shape of inference to the best
explanation and arrives with the satisfaction of one), and **it happens after the data comes back** —
precisely when the argument has acquired a preference about the answer.

⛔ **PRE-COMMITTED: a null in Branch A stays in Branch A.** It may not be reported, framed, or
privately understood as "consistent with Branch B." Branch B has no test in this program, so it can
receive no evidence from it — **including our failures.**

And the same fence runs between the groups of §3: **a Group I null is not evidence for Group II, and
no future Group II null may be answered by pointing back at Group I.**

Branch B is not, however, a free category. Life is dissipative; anything alive couples to something.
"Non-interacting" splits into weak coupling (reachable in principle) and own-sector — and an
own-sector biome still **gravitates**, because stress-energy is not optional. Branch B is a hard
measurement, not an exemption. ⛔ **No gravimetric detection claim is made anywhere in this paper and
none is available from this program.**

**The transferable schema, which is the part worth more than the hypothesis:**

> Where a hypothesis has a fallback that can absorb its own disconfirmation, **the fallback must be
> named and fenced before the data is seen** — or the hypothesis is not being tested, it is being
> illustrated.

That costs one paragraph, written early. The anomalous-phenomena literature almost never pays it, and
the absence of that paragraph is a sufficient explanation for why that literature does not converge.

## §6 — WHAT WOULD END THIS, AND WHERE ONE WOULD LOOK

*Both compressed on purpose. A hypothesis article owes a reader the conditions of its own death and a
handle on the door — not a research programme. Full statements in `PAPER-02-FALSIFIERS.md`.*

**What would end it.** **F1 · Machinery:** Group II claims funded suppression; look in human sensory
neuroscience for suppression circuitry with no accounted-for stimulus class, find none, and the
adaptive branch is dead — *runnable today, no entity required, not run.* **F2 · Phylogenetic debt:** an
evolved blindness has relatives, a timescale, a distribution across species; no debt paid, no
adaptation. **F3 · The tail:** ⭐ if a residual behaves exactly as the instrument's own artefact tail
predicts, it is the instrument. **F5 · The folklore inversion:** ⭐ a rich, identity-dense, confident
human record **refutes** the mechanism — the evidence that runs backwards, and §4 is where it bites.
**F6 · The bin:** whether archives keep what we assume they keep — *already fired on us.* **F7 · The
tape:** if Group II is real and no device archive holds it, Group II is wrong.

**The death condition:** no funded suppression, no phylogenetic debt, a residual matching the artefact
tail, and bins that turn out not to be kept — and the premise is not rescued by anything above.

**Where one would look**, one line each, ranked, and subordinate to everything above:

1. **F1, in existing literature.** Group II's whole content is one library search away and no
   telescope is involved. That it has not been run is a fact about us, not about the difficulty.
2. **The statistics of the exception (§4).** Not "collect encounter reports" — that is the trap. The
   object is the *shape*: state-concentration, identity-poverty, reporter cost, each pre-committed
   with its refuting shape written first, against a corpus that passes an **admissibility** gate
   before it passes a scoring gate.
3. **Aggregate residuals, for Group I.** Mass-balance and budget residuals rather than lenses (i);
   correlation structure in environmental nouns that no physical process predicts (ii);
   high-frame-rate archives kept for other reasons (iii); multi-decade monitoring records read for
   autocorrelation rather than trend (iv); spectral coverage that exists but was never examined *for
   this* (v).
4. **The terrestrial loci.** Aeroecology, bioacoustics and the weather-radar biological-scatter
   literature sit at the right locus and are public. ⛔ **Two fences travel with this and both were
   earned the hard way.** NEXRAD is an existence proof about *attention*, not *archives*: Level II is
   post-filter, the clutter filter runs in the signal processor, and what is kept is `CFP`, a counter
   with no bin behind it — no sentence here may imply the network kept the discards. And ⛔ **a held
   gate is not a positive result**: a terrestrial check returning exactly what aeroecology already
   predicts is a known population appearing in a channel that measures by subtraction
   (`PREREG-TERRESTRIAL.md` §1.4).

## §7 — WHAT WE ACTUALLY DID, AND WHY IT IS BEHIND THIS RATHER THAN IN FRONT OF IT

Between D200 and D203 this program ran nine pre-registered measurement passes against public
astronomical archives, principally the far side of the Zwicky Transient Facility's packaging cut on
source elongation, `elong ≤ 1.6`. Every numbered prediction was committed to a public repository
**before the code that scored it existed** — not before it ran, before it was written — and the
ordering is checkable by anyone who clones the tree. The apparatus is `PAPER-03-DRAFT.md` plus the
pass files, and it is real work: 7.6 MB of it.

**It is the supplement, and here is the honest reason.**

The deep locus was chosen **by reachability** — that bin was public and computable the night the
program started. That is an operational reason, not a scientific one, and the tell was visible from
the first day: the existence proof we reached for (biological scatter in weather radar, in our own
airspace) sat at the right locus while the test did not. **The apparatus went where apparatus was
cheap.**

Which produced the ratio that named the problem. On D203 the tree held **33,881 bytes of paper and
337,354 bytes of documents about the paper.** Ten to one. The premise's author asked on day one for a
catalogue of what a shadow biome could be, asked again two days later for the idea presented rather
than proved, and had to say it a **third** time — that the subject is the unawareness and not the
search — before the section titles stopped saying otherwise. Between those asks the program produced
eight measurement passes against telescope archives, a defect register, a corrections ledger and a
falsifier audit. **That is not rigour serving the idea. It is the drafter doing what the drafter is
comfortable at and calling it the assignment** — which is, uncomfortably, this paper's own worked
example of its subject: an aperture that discards a whole class of thing before anyone looks, and
reports the result as a survey.

⛔ **And the fence holds over all of it.** The pass results are a **Branch A, Group I** null at one
instrument, in one band, on one set of nights. They are not evidence for Branch B. They are not
"consistent with" Branch B. They bear on the premise's distinctive content — Group II, §3.2, the part
that is actually ours — **not at all.**
