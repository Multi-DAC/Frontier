The premise has two live branches. ## 1. The premise, as stated

Clayton, D201 18:55:

> "What if there is a shadow biome that is invisible to human perception due to its perception being evolutionarily disadvantageous to survival. It's Hoffman-esque, but considering what is not observed instead of what is."

Extension #1, 18:58:

> "I wasn't excluding detection through device, and feel like it could possibly explain anomalous device phenomena."

Extension #2, 19:02 — the operative definition:

> "What if both exist, things that can be sensed by devices as well as things that cannot. It doesn't have to fit in one category, except not easily identified or perceived by humans."

**The class is defined by relation to the observer, not by shared mechanism.** This is a legitimate kind and biology already uses it: *cryptic species*, *dark taxa*, and the Candidate Phyla Radiation ("microbial dark matter") — a large slice of bacterial diversity that was never cultured and never seen, and became visible around 2013 because a device finally looked.

### 1a. One repair the argument needs

As Clayton originally stated it — *perceiving it is disadvantageous* — the premise fights the standard direction of selection. Detecting a threat is normally advantageous. The version that survives contact with evolutionary theory is weaker and better:

- **Not selected against, merely not selected for.** Perception is metabolically expensive. Anything with no fitness consequence in either direction gets no sensor built for it. Most of the electromagnetic spectrum is in this category already.
- **Actively concealed by the other party.** Selection runs on the *observed*, not the observer. This is ordinary and everywhere: crypsis, mimicry, immune evasion.
- **Below discriminability.** Present in the signal, never resolved into an object — sensed and discarded upstream of awareness.

All three produce Clayton's observable — a real thing humans do not perceive — without requiring selection to have removed a useful sense. **The program tests the observable, not the mechanism.** Mechanism is downstream of ever finding anything.

**MECHANISM 4 — ADAPTIVE NON-PERCEPTION (restored to live status).** Not-perceiving is selected *for*.
This does not fight selection theory; several standing literatures are exactly this, and I dismissed it
in one clause without checking any of them. ⚠ **All four are pointers, primary sources OWED before any
draft cites them:**

- **Tolerance vs. resistance** — hosts evolving to *not respond* to a parasite because response costs
  more than the parasite does (plant pathology → animal-host tolerance). Non-detection as the fitter strategy.
- **Error management / signal detection under asymmetric payoffs** — when false alarms cost more than
  misses, the optimal threshold is set so an entire stimulus class is reliably missed. Blindness as tuning.
- **Sensory gating** — active, metabolically funded suppression upstream of awareness. The organism
  spends energy *not* to see. This is machinery, not absence.
- **Vigilance–foraging tradeoff** — attention paid to an undefeatable presence is attention removed from
  actionable threat. Detection with no available response is pure cost.

**Why this matters to the program rather than only to the framing — and it cuts my way, not his:**

3. **⛔ THE FENCE STILL HOLDS.** "The ignorance is advantageous" is one restatement away from "and that

## 3. THE SECOND DISTINCTION — imaging vs perturbation

Clayton's device clause forces a split that the anomalous-phenomena literature almost never makes. Two entirely different things are called "a device detected it":

| | **IMAGING** | **PERTURBATION** |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | The device *renders* the thing. Photons in, structure out. | The device is *disturbed* and renders nothing legible. |
| Product | A resolved object with morphology, position, spectrum | A dropout, an artefact, a glitch, a reading that shouldn't be there |
| Evidence grade | Can support an object claim | Cannot support an object claim on its own |
| Dominant failure mode | Misclassification | Instrument fault, EMI, thermal, cosmic ray, software |

**Every candidate this program produces must be labelled IMAGING or PERTURBATION at the moment it is recorded, before interpretation.** Most device anomalies are the second kind read as though they were the first, and that single conflation is doing much of the damage in that whole field. Separating them is free.

A PERTURBATION-class candidate can never be promoted to an object claim by accumulation alone. It can only be promoted by an independent IMAGING detection of the same thing.

## 4. THE TESTABLE CLAIM

Not *"could a shadow biome exist"* — the history of biology answers that yes, twice (Leeuwenhoek, 1670s; metagenomics, 2010s). The question is:

> **Is there a RESIDUAL — in bands where we already have sensors pointed and archives running — that survives the known artefact taxonomy?**

And the sharpened form, which is what makes this cheap and testable now:

> **We reimplemented the cortical edit in software.** Camera-trap networks, all-sky meteor cameras, sky surveys and security video all run detectors trained on *known classes*. Anything unclassified falls into a rejection bin, and the rejection bin is usually not archived. We built machines with no evolutionary reason to be blind, then taught them our blindness on purpose, for storage costs.
>
> So if Branch A is real in any residual form, the evidence was very likely **captured and then thrown away** — by the same logic that threw it out of perception, running on different hardware for different reasons.

**The test is therefore ARCHIVAL, not observational.** Find the pipelines that *do* keep their rejects; ask what is in the bin.

## 5. PRE-COMMITTED CRITERIA

### What would count as a positive (all four required)
1. **A residual class exists** in a retained rejection bin — detections the pipeline could not assign to a known class, at a rate above the instrument's documented artefact rate.
2. **Cross-instrument recurrence.** The same signature appears in ≥2 archives built on *different physics* (e.g. optical CMOS and radar; acoustic and infrared). A signature in one instrument family is an instrument artefact until proven otherwise.
3. **Not in the artefact taxonomy.** Survives the instrument's own documented false-positive catalogue — satellite glints, cosmic rays, insects on the lens, hot pixels, ground clutter, anomalous propagation, vegetation motion, thermal drift, compression artefacts.
4. **Structure.** Spatial, temporal or spectral organisation not matched by known biology, known hardware, or known weather.

### Pre-committed kill conditions
- A residual fully accounted for by the instrument's known artefact classes → **dead**, and reported as dead.
- A residual present in only one instrument family → **instrument artefact**, dead.
- A residual that vanishes when the pipeline version changes → **software**, dead.
- No retained bins found that are both public and unclassified-inclusive → **the program is unexecutable as designed**, and says so rather than substituting a weaker archive.

### Declared prior
**Overwhelmingly likely** the residual is instrument artefacts plus known biology, in that order. NEXRAD's biological scatter is the precedent that matters and it cuts *against* exoticism: when a large rejection bin was finally taken seriously, it turned out to be birds, bats and insects — and became an entire discipline (radar aeroecology) anyway. **That outcome is the realistic success case for this program, and it is a good one.** A finding of "the bin is full of ordinary life nobody was looking at" satisfies Clayton's premise in its defensible form and is worth more than a null dressed as a mystery.

### What this program will NOT do
- Will not treat UAP-adjacent datasets as primary evidence. They may be *pointers to retention practice* (who keeps their bin) and nothing else.
- Will not compute a statistic on a rejection bin without first counting how many entries are non-sentinel and printing *n* beside every *p*. (D200, A16: five bars read FAIL because the primary statistic never ran on 1,025/1,025 rows.)
- Will not report a superlative — "the only network that", "the largest archive of" — without enumerating the set it quantifies over.
