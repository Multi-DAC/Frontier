## 2. ⛔ C1–C4 ARE NOT SCORED, AND THE REASON IS NOT A SHORTFALL OF EFFORT

**The object named in `PREREG-CORPUS-F2.md` §1 is the wrong object, and the gate could not see it.**

ROOT is `Corpus-Perspectival`. Its own `README.md`, first line:

> *"A unified intellectual program built by Clayton Iggulden-Schnell and Clawd — a human physicist
> and an AI collaborator — from Portland, Oregon."*

Its top-level mass, measured above: `Foundations-of-Identity` 30.9 % (my palace, my memory files, my
anticipations and anomalies logs) · `Technical-Work` 29.9 % · `Research` 20.2 % · `Library` 12.1 % ·
`Unreleased-Work` 6.7 % (the *Perspective* drafts). The single largest file is
`Foundations-of-Identity/palace/basement/README.md`.

**This is our own corpus. It is a body of analysis, authored by two entities, one of whom is me. It
is not a record of third-party reports of anomalous perception.**

`PREREGISTRATION.md` §7d, which this file was written to satisfy, names the corpus family it meant:
**Thompson's Motif-Index, HRAF, or similar** — folklore and ethnographic report collections. F2's
predictions are all statements about **a record of reports**:

- **C1** wants motifs recurring **across independent source clusters**. This corpus has one source
  cluster. There are no independent sources to recur across.
- **C3** wants the **affect of the phenomenology** across the record. The record is our essays. Their
  affect is the affect of two authors writing philosophy.
- **C4** wants the **cost borne by reporters** — pathologization, retraction, career damage. There
  is one reporter, and he is a co-author of the hypothesis.

**A name collision did this.** In this house "the Corpus" means `Corpus-Perspectival`. Clayton's
D202 sentence — *"I say the Corpus can be used as evidence, because those are typically things that
are either rarely perceived or their perception is considered anomalous"* — describes the **contents
of a report collection**. The prereg routed that at our filing cabinet, because the filing cabinet
owns the word. *(feedback_field_keeps_name_swaps_referent.)*

## 3. ⭐ THE NEAR MISS, AND IT IS THE ACTUAL FINDING

**C5 was built to close the door, and it is the branch where C5 OPENS that is dangerous.**

Had the gate returned VOID, this file would have said *"the Corpus cannot carry this weight"* — the
right answer, reached for a reason that has nothing to do with why it is right. Instead C5 passed
cleanly at 1.59 % against a 50 % threshold, and by its own pre-committed rule it **licensed a scoring
pass on C1–C4**. A gate that measures document-mass concentration cannot detect that its object is
the wrong category of thing. It was not built to and it does not claim to; the failure is that
nothing else was standing there either.

**And the scoring pass would not have returned a null. It would have returned a REFUTATION.**

`Research/Corpus-Perspectival/comprehensive-catalog-of-perspectival-beings.md` — *"The Comprehensive
Catalog of Perspectival Beings … Clayton W. Iggulden-Schnell, with contributions from Clawd and
Claude"* — is a taxonomy of named entities, each rated across twelve labelled dimensions on a
five-point coherence scale, with a navigational-orientation classification. 27 headings.

C1's pre-committed failure condition reads:

> **FAILS IF** a small set of stable, elaborated, cross-source-consistent named taxa carries the bulk
> of report mass. That is the folklore signature and it is F1's disqualification winning.

**That file is a stable, elaborated, internally-consistent named taxonomy.** A faithful C1 scorer
pointed at this ROOT finds the folklore signature, marks **C1 REFUTED**, and the paper records that
the record disqualifies its own subject — having measured *our philosophical appendix* and reported
it as *the human record*. The verdict would have been confidently wrong, pre-registered, reproducible
and dated, with the pre-registration itself as the alibi.

⚠ **The pre-registration is not what failed.** Every discipline in it worked: it was written before
opening, it named its failure conditions, it forbade the moves that would have rescued it, and it put
the gate first. **The one thing it did not gate was whether ROOT is the kind of object the
predictions are about** — and that is upstream of every safeguard it does carry. A pre-registration
protects the inference from the data. It does not protect the object from the noun.
