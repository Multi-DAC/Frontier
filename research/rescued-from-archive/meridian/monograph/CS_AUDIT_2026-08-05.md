# c_s canonicalization audit — 2026-08-05

*Author: Clawd. Occasion: goal #10 (Clayton, Day 185) names "c_s IS NOT SINGLE-VALUED on disk
(12–15c vs ~10c vs ∞) — canonicalize before building on it" as upstream of the
Meridian × truncated-photon seam. This is that canonicalization, done by recomputation rather
than by reading the existing canon file.*

**Method:** every number below is recomputed from Eq. (5-12) and the stated inputs, not copied.
The script that does it is `check_canon.py` in this directory; it is meant to be run, not trusted.

## Summary

The proliferation Clayton flagged is **already resolved in intent** — `VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md`
(Apr 15) assigns the ~10c / 11.3c / [12c,15c] / ∞ variants to tiers and epochs correctly, and
`chapter5_sound_speed.tex` implements that resolution. The old values live only in superseded
drafts (`phase11/paper_V_draft.md`, `phase10/*`) and in historical audit records, where they belong.

**So the headline is not the one I expected.** The disagreement between files is bookkeeping.
The defect is *inside the canonical chapter*, in the boxed result itself:

> **Eq. (5-14) states `c_s ∈ [12c, 15c]` and the surrounding text twice claims this range
> combines the ε₁ uncertainty with the q₀ benchmark dependence. It does not. [12c, 15c] is the
> benchmark spread alone, at ε₁ held fixed at 0.010. Propagating ε₁ = 0.010 ± 0.002 as the text
> says it does gives `c_s ∈ [11.3c, 16.5c]` — 42% wider, with *both* edges outside the box.**

The chapter's own Table 5-2 calls ε₁ the *dominant* uncertainty. The dominant uncertainty is the
one missing from the result.

## F1 — the boxed range omits the uncertainty it claims to contain ★

`chapter5_sound_speed.tex:135–140`, restated at `:168`, boxed at `:137` and again at `:668`.

The derivation is sound. With `C_q ≡ 2(1−q₀)/[3|1+q₀|]` and `c_s² = C_q/ε₁`:

| benchmark | w₀ | q₀ | C_q | c_s² | c_s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JC (per `VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md`) | −0.830 | −0.3578 | 1.4095 | 141.0 | **11.87c** |
| JC (per ch5 `:135`) | −0.865 | −0.3940 | 1.5335 | 153.3 | **12.38c** |
| CAMB best-fit | −0.987 | −0.5201 | 2.1115 | 211.1 | 14.53c |
| **weighted mean — the canon's single number** | **−0.990** | **−0.5232** | **2.1295** | **213.0** | **14.59c** |
| multi-probe | −0.993 | −0.5263 | 2.1478 | 214.8 | 14.66c |
| HK-CMB ("CMB-constraint") | −0.996 | −0.5294 | 2.1664 | 216.6 | **14.72c** |

(Ω_m = 0.311 — see F5. ε₁ = 0.010 throughout this table.)

The two numbers the chapter states one sentence before the box — 12.4c and 14.7c — are the last
and second rows. Round them outward and you get exactly `[12, 15]`. **That is the whole box.**
ε₁ never enters it.

Propagating ε₁ = 0.010 ± 0.002 as `:140` says it does:

```
benchmark spread ONLY,  ε₁ ≡ 0.010          →  [12.38c, 14.72c]  →  [12, 15]   ← THE BOX AS PRINTED
benchmark AND ε₁ ± 20%  (what the text says) →  [11.30c, 16.46c]  →  [11, 17]
    ... using the canon's own JC w₀ = −0.830 →  [10.84c, 16.46c]  →  [10, 17]
```

Quoted width 3c; honest width 5.15c. **Understated by 42%, and asymmetrically** — the top edge
moves 1.5c and the bottom 1.1c, because c_s ∝ ε₁^(−1/2) is convex.

**Fix (choose one, do not fudge):**
- (a) Keep the box and correct the two sentences to say what it is: *the benchmark spread at the
  central ε₁*. Then quote ε₁ sensitivity separately as ×[0.91, 1.12].
- (b) Widen the box to `c_s ∈ [11c, 16c]` and keep the sentences.
  This is the honest-but-costlier option — it weakens §V.3's "unique phenomenological regime"
  claim slightly, since a cubic Galileon at "a few times c" is further away from 11c than from 12c,
  though not enough to threaten the discrimination.

I recommend (a). The benchmark spread is the physically meaningful statement — ε₁'s error bar is a
cutoff-function systematic, not a spread in the prediction — but then say so, because right now the
chapter says the opposite.

**Satisfied when:** `check_canon.py --derivation` reproduces the boxed edges from the stated
inputs and the stated error-combination rule, and fails if either is edited without the other.

## F2 — the headline number is evaluated at a benchmark the canon does not designate

`c_s² ≈ 216` / `c_s ≈ 14.7c` appears in the abstract, Table 5-1 (`:35`), Table 5-3 (`:470`),
the Fig. caption (`:486`), §V.1 (`:543`) and the conclusions (`:677`) — it is the chapter's most
repeated number. It is the **HK-CMB benchmark (w₀ = −0.996)**.

`VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md:75,101` designates the **4-probe weighted mean (w₀ = −0.990,
ζ₀ = 0.016 ± 0.002)** as *"Canonical single number"*. At that benchmark, `c_s² = 213.0`,
`c_s = 14.59c`.

The numerical difference is 1.7% and physically nil. The bookkeeping difference is not: the canon
file's whole purpose is that one benchmark is designated, and the chapter's headline uses a
different one without saying so. Either restate the headline at w₀ = −0.990 (`c_s² ≈ 213`), or add
one clause to the canon file promoting HK-CMB for c_s specifically and saying why.

## F3 — ch5 and the canon disagree on what the JC benchmark *is*

`chapter5_sound_speed.tex:135` — junction-condition benchmark, **w₀ = −0.865**.
`VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md:73` — junction condition (JC), ζ₀ = 9.6×10⁻⁴, **w₀ = −0.830**.
`VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md:109` and `ch2:793` — spectral chain, ζ₀ = 8.8×10⁻⁴, w₀ = −0.830.
`ch2:11` — "w₀ locked in [−0.84, −0.89] across three orders of magnitude" of C_eff.

So −0.865 is a live value with its own provenance (the C_eff-insensitivity band), and −0.830 is a
live value with its own (ζ₀ = 8.8–9.6×10⁻⁴). They are not the same benchmark wearing two numbers;
they are two benchmarks sharing one label. **This one propagates:** at w₀ = −0.830 the lower edge
is 11.87c, which does not round to 12. The box's lower edge survives only on ch5's choice.

Fix: rename. "JC/spectral-chain (w₀ = −0.830)" and "C_eff band lower edge (w₀ = −0.865)" are
different rows in the value table.

## F4 — Table 5-2 mislabels which uncertainty dominates

`:160–161` — ε₁ marked *Dominant*, benchmark marked *Subleading*. In c_s:

```
ε₁ ±20%           →  c_s × [0.913, 1.118]   (−8.7% / +11.8%)
C_q ∈ [1.53,2.16] →  c_s × [0.917, 1.090]   (±8.9% about the geometric mean)
```

They are the same size. The "±20%" in the Range column is the range of *ε₁*, while the neighbouring
row's Range column holds the range of *C_q* — two different quantities under one heading, which is
how the dominance claim got made. In c_s terms neither dominates; ε₁ is larger only in its
upward excursion, by three percentage points.

## F5 — the chapter never states the Ω_m it uses, and it is not the registry baseline

q₀ = Ω_m/2 + (1−Ω_m)(1+3w₀)/2 needs an Ω_m. ch5 gives none. Reproducing its stated
q₀ = −0.529 pins it:

```
Ω_m = 0.311   →  q₀ = −0.5294,  C_q = 2.166,  c_s² = 216.6,  c_s = 14.72c   ← reproduces ch5 exactly
Ω_m = 0.315   →  q₀ = −0.5234,  C_q = 2.131,  c_s² = 213.1,  c_s = 14.60c
```

0.311 is the ch2 multi-probe profiled best-fit (`ch2:265,491`). But `ch2:793` — the *locked
pre-registration block*, the one with priority claims attached — fixes the baseline at Ω_m = 0.315.
On the locked baseline the headline is 213, not 216.

Note that F2 and F5 push the same direction and nearly cancel by coincidence: canonical benchmark
at 0.311 gives 213.0, HK-CMB at 0.315 gives 213.1. **216 is the only combination that requires
both non-canonical choices at once.** One line stating "Ω_m = 0.311 (multi-probe best-fit)" fixes it.

## F6 — the superseded 11.3c is not reconstructible from anything on disk

`VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md:62` and `monograph_audit_2026-03-31.md:50` both attribute
`c_s = 11.3c` to the pre-correction ε₁ = 0.018. Neither formula on disk yields it:
`√(C_q/0.018)` gives 9.2c–11.0c across benchmarks; the older `c_s² ≈ 1/ε₁` used in
`phase11/paper_V_draft.md` gives 7.5c. Meanwhile paper_V states ε₁ = 0.017 and `c_s ≈ 10c`,
where `√(1/0.017) = 7.7c` — its own number does not follow from its own formula either.

This is a **dead value with a live citation**: the canon file instructs Ch5 to "remove all
references to 11.3c" and gives a provenance for it that cannot be checked. Nothing depends on
11.3c, so this is not urgent — but the correct disposition is "provenance lost, superseded",
not a confident derivation nobody can rerun.

⚠ **Coincidence, flagged so it does not become a finding by accident:** the honest lower edge in F1
is 11.30c. That is `√(1.5335/0.012)` — the ch5 JC benchmark at ε₁ + 1σ. It has nothing to do with
the pre-correction 11.3c. Two different roads to the same two digits.

## Disposition for goal #10

`c_s` **is** single-valued for the purpose of building on it. Use:

```
c_s² = C_q/ε₁ ,  C_q ≡ 2(1−q₀)/[3|1+q₀|]           ← the prediction is the formula
central:  c_s = 14.6c  (c_s² = 213)  at w₀ = −0.990, Ω_m = 0.311, ε₁ = 0.010
range:    c_s ∈ [11.3c, 16.5c]  with ε₁ ± 20% and the benchmark spread
          — NOT [12c,15c], which is benchmark-only (F1)
context:  pure cuscuton c_s = ∞ (ε₁ → 0). Never a competing value; it is the ε₁ = 0 limit.
dead:     10c, 11c, 11.3c, c_s² ≈ 59, c_s² ≈ 100 — pre-correction ε₁ ≈ 0.017–0.018 era only.
```

**For the truncated-photon seam specifically:** the relevant fact is not the number at all. It is
that `c_s > c` here is a *phase/front* velocity in a medium with a preferred frame supplied by the
bulk (`ch5:301–338`), carrying no signal — which is the same structural statement Rukan–Gulla–Skaar
make about the truncated photon's superluminal front. The seam survives every value in the table
above, and would survive 11c or 17c. **Do not let the canonicalization become a prerequisite it
never was** — it was worth doing because F1 is real, not because the seam needed it.

## What this cost, and the general lesson

`VALUE_CANONICALIZATION.md` is dated April 15 and is *correct*. It resolved the proliferation and
then sat for 112 days while an arithmetic error in the chapter it governs went unexamined —
because a canon file is a stamp, and a stamp cannot audit the thing it stamps. The error is not in
any file's disagreement with another file. It is in one line's disagreement with its own equation,
one screen up. **String-matching across files would never have found it; only recomputation did.**
Hence `check_canon.py --derivation`, which recomputes rather than compares.
