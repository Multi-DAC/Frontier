**Named.** The counterintelligence technique is the *barium meal* (Clancy's phrase; the practice is older: unique-detail salting of each recipient's copy, the "canary trap" in publishing). Clayton's proposal is its inversion, run by the reader rather than the leak-hunter. Structurally it is consensus alignment: many noisy reads of one sequence, keep the column majority, discard the singletons.

**My position, stated before the objections (as the D203 rule requires):** it works, and it is the right frame for the leak/testimony register of the Frontier program. ## 2. The identifiability problem

The method keys on one signature, divergence, and assumes it has one cause. It has three, and they are not distinguishable from the divergence alone.

| what produced the divergent claim | what it looks like in the leak | what culling it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Poison** (a salted false detail, unique to one compartment) | unique to one source, confidently stated, does not connect to anything else in the account | correct: removes a lie |
| **Partial view** (the one thing that compartment alone saw) | unique to one source, confidently stated, *connected* to the rest of the account by mechanism or timeline | wrong: removes the only true detail nobody else could have |
| **Leaker overreach** (gap-filling from the public corpus; Gerb's own diagnosis of the 4chan OP) | unique to one source, matches a *pre-existing folklore item* | correct in effect, but for the wrong reason: the detail was never in any compartment |

Clayton himself supplied the second row an hour earlier: *"the information each displays is non-corroborating through compartmentalization."* That sentence and the poison model make opposite predictions about the same divergence. Both are true of some cells; the matrix has to say which.

Convergence has the mirror problem. Two leaks agree because (a) they saw the same truth, (b) one read the other, (c) both drew from the same folklore, or (d) both were fed the same injection. (d) is not hypothetical in this field: Bennewitz/Doty 1980–83 (AFOSI-fed underground-base and treaty narratives), the MJ-12 papers (1984, Moore/Shandera, same circle), Serpo (2005–06, "Anonymous" via Doty's mailing list). Any two post-1985 sources agreeing on Grays, treaties, underground bases, or exchange programmes converge on an *injection*, and the method would synthesise it into "the actual case". So agreement is not free either. It has to be priced.

## 3. The refinement

1. **Claim-level, not source-level.** Rows are atomic claims. Columns are sources. Cells are ASSERTS / DENIES / SILENT. *Silence is a compartment edge, not poison, and not agreement.* A source that never addresses a claim votes nothing.
2. **Independence graph first.** Before any cell is scored, draw the edges: who read whom (measured, not assumed), who shares a reporter, a lawyer, a podcast circuit, a publisher. Agreement across an edge counts once, not twice.
3. **Convergence weighted by surprisal under the folklore prior.** Agreement on a claim that was already in the public corpus when the earlier source spoke carries ~0 information. Agreement on a claim absent from the corpus at that time is the only convergence worth synthesising.
4. **Divergence scored by edge-count to other claims in the same account.** A divergent claim that connects to nothing else the source says (a *leaf*) is poison-shaped or overreach-shaped; check it against folklore to split those two. A divergent claim that other claims in the same account depend on (a *node*) is partial-view-shaped and is the last thing to cull.
5. **A contradiction needs a third independent vote** before either side is called false. Two compartments disagreeing is the expected observation under both hypotheses.
6. **A divergence that runs *against* the folklore prior is the rarest kind and gets flagged, not culled.** Overreach imports folklore; it never contradicts it. So an anti-folklore divergence can only be poison or partial view, and the edge-count in rule 4 is what separates them.

### What the first pass says, in order of what it settles

1. **The cells everyone agrees on carry nothing** (rows 1, 8, 9, 12). Under the naive method these are the synthesised "actual case". Under the refinement they are the folklore prior reflected back, and row 8 is the 1980s injection specifically. This is the concrete cost of running the method without the prior.
2. **The two contradictions with compartment claims on both sides** (rows 5, 6: destruction, interception) have no third vote and stay open. The OP's versions are nodes; Gerb's are leaves resting on unnamed engineers. Neither is culled.
3. **The one divergence the method can classify with confidence** is row 12a: the OP's denial that triangles exist. It is anti-folklore, so it is not overreach, and it is a node, so by rule 4 it is partial-view-shaped rather than poison-shaped. Whether it is *true* is a separate question the matrix cannot answer; what it can say is that this is the OP's most compartment-shaped statement, and nobody in the thread, the r/UFOs comments, or Gerb's two hours noticed it.
4. **The best convergence** is row 9a (Fukushima, weeks/months, between the two least-connected columns), with row 11 (human activity draws craft, three chains, three polarities) behind it. Both are medium-prior, so they are worth something, not everything.
5. **Row 14 is where the canary-trap reading and the compartment reading make the same prediction** and cannot be separated from inside the matrix: four dated "turns to secrecy", four dates. Gerb–Elizondo's 2010/2011 near-agreement across no edge is the only structure in it.
